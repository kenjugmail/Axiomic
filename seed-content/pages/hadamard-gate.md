# Hadamard Gate

The most-used single-qubit gate. Creates equal superposition from a basis state.

`H = (1/√2)[[1, 1], [1, -1]]`

`H|0⟩ = |+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2`
`H|1⟩ = |−⟩ = (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2`

`HH = I`. The Hadamard is its own inverse.

Almost every quantum algorithm starts with H gates: put qubits into superposition, apply problem-specific operations, then post-process.

<!-- tier:undergrad -->
# Hadamard Gate (Undergrad)

## What H does

H maps the computational basis {|0⟩, |1⟩} to the X-basis {|+⟩, |−⟩}.

- `H|0⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2` — equal superposition.
- `H|1⟩ = (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2` — equal superposition with relative phase.
- `H|+⟩ = |0⟩`. H is involutive: HH = I.
- `H|−⟩ = |1⟩`.

Geometrically: 180° rotation about the axis (x + z)/√2 on the Bloch sphere.

## Hadamard on n qubits

Applying H to each of n qubits creates uniform superposition over all `2^n` basis states:

`H^⊗n |0⟩^n = (1/√(2^n)) Σ_x |x⟩`

This is the standard 'preparation' step in quantum algorithms (Deutsch-Jozsa, Grover, Simon, Shor, etc.). After preparing this superposition, an oracle or unitary processes all `2^n` states simultaneously.

This 'evaluation in parallel' is the source of quantum advantage — combined with interference at the end.

## H as basis change

H performs a basis change between Z and X bases:
- Z-basis: |0⟩, |1⟩.
- X-basis: |+⟩, |−⟩.
- `H Z H = X`. H rotates Z observable to X.

Measurement in the X basis = apply H, measure in Z. This is how all measurements in non-computational bases are implemented on hardware that only measures in Z.

## Relation to Fourier transform

For one qubit, H is the discrete Fourier transform (modulo phases). The quantum Fourier transform on `n` qubits generalizes: it's NOT just `H^⊗n`, but H ⊗ ... × controlled phase rotations.

The Hadamard is the simplest QFT instance.

## H in algorithms

- **Deutsch-Jozsa**: H prepares + post-processes.
- **Grover**: H prepares uniform superposition; diffusion operator includes H gates.
- **Simon**: H pre/post-processing reveals hidden XOR.
- **Shor**: H gates set up the counting register before modular exponentiation.
- **VQE / QAOA**: H gates initialize ansätze.

H is everywhere in quantum algorithms.

# Hadamard Gate (Grad)

## Hadamard + S generate single-qubit Clifford group

The single-qubit Clifford group is generated by {H, S}. Combined with CNOT (which is itself in the Clifford group on 2 qubits), {H, S, CNOT} generates the full multi-qubit Clifford group.

Clifford circuits are classically simulable (Gottesman-Knill). So H + S alone are not universal — need T or other non-Clifford gates.

## Hadamard test

A standard subroutine: estimate `Re ⟨ψ|U|ψ⟩` for a unitary `U` and state `|ψ⟩`.

**Procedure**:
1. Prepare an ancilla in `|0⟩`; system in `|ψ⟩`.
2. Apply H to ancilla: `(|0⟩ + |1⟩)|ψ⟩/√2`.
3. Apply controlled-U (control = ancilla, target = system): `(|0⟩|ψ⟩ + |1⟩U|ψ⟩)/√2`.
4. Apply H to ancilla.
5. Measure ancilla. P(0) − P(1) = Re ⟨ψ|U|ψ⟩.

This is a foundational subroutine in many quantum algorithms (VQE, phase estimation).

## H in error correction

H plays a role in stabilizer codes:
- The 7-qubit Steane code's logical H is transversal (apply H to each physical qubit).
- The surface code's logical H requires lattice surgery — non-trivial.

The transversality of H (vs T) makes Clifford error correction cheap; T-gate distillation is expensive.

## H + decoherence

A fresh |0⟩ in superposition |+⟩ = H|0⟩ has the relative phase between |0⟩ and |1⟩ matters. Decoherence destroys this relative phase, turning |+⟩ into a maximally-mixed state in the X-basis (still a 50/50 mixture in the Z-basis but no longer coherent).

T₂ (dephasing time) characterizes how fast this happens. Algorithms using H must complete within T₂.
